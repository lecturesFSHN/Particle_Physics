## 1. Purpose and scope

Design, construct, commission, and operate a small particle physics experiment. Formulate a question that can be answered by recorded observables, and produce an analysis with uncertainties and explicit limits to interpretation. Reproducing an established result or testing a detection principle is acceptable; novelty is not required.

Buying a finished instrument without substantial student design, integration, commissioning, and measurement is insufficient. Existing detector and acquisition components may be reused. Explain what your team will build and what it will learn.

## 2. Available resources

The department reports access to plastic scintillators or crystals such as NaI, photomultipliers, a multichannel analyzer, a DAQ board with amplification, discrimination/threshold selection, TDC and FPGA/microcontroller counting, an electronics laboratory, neodymium magnets, and lead plates. Instructor technical guidance is available.

Before relying on a resource, confirm its quantity, dimensions, condition, booking, interface, and specifications. In particular, verify PMT bases and compatible high-voltage supplies, DAQ input limits and timing capabilities, and the availability of tools and consumables. Do not assume the MCA provides event timestamps or that the DAQ can simultaneously capture all proposed observables. Instructor guidance supports student work; it does not replace it.

## 3. Application

Use [the application form](APPLICATION_TEMPLATE.md). Maximum six rendered A4 pages for the main narrative (sections 1–6, including figures); cover details, budget, references, and technical appendices are additional. For Markdown submissions also submit a PDF export solely to establish pagination. Use legible text, approximately 11-point or larger, and normal margins. Essential feasibility evidence belongs in the main narrative.

Include a precise question, apparatus diagram, original construction work, at least one quantitative feasibility calculation, an expected-result plot labelled as a prediction, commissioning criteria, analysis and uncertainty plans, work allocation, risks, a minimum achievable result, and a justified budget. Support scientific claims and component choices with references and datasheets. Separate known facts, assumptions, and quantities to be measured.

## 4. Funding and expenditure

The ceiling is a limit, not a spending target. Existing resources are institutional contributions and must not be charged again. Request only necessary incremental costs. No student salary is budgeted in this teaching call. Include delivery, taxes and import charges where applicable, and identify whether estimates include them. Any reserve must have an explicit purpose; an unspent reserve is not an expense.

Submit dated quotations or traceable price estimates before purchase. Purchases require instructor authorization and must follow the institution's actual procedures, which the instructor will supply. Do not commit personal funds on the assumption of reimbursement. The educational financial form is not a substitute for institutional accounting.

Record changes in scientific scope, equipment, milestones, and budget in a short change request before the affected purchase or activity. Transfers between approved budget lines require instructor agreement. The approved total cannot be exceeded without a revised award. Ownership and handover arrangements: [Specify institution's rules].

## 5. Milestones and deliverables

| Stage | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Proposal review | Application, budget, group pitch, individual questions |
| Construction | Drawings, schematics, code, assembly records and logbook |
| Commissioning | Signals and performance checks demonstrating fitness for the planned measurement |
| Physics measurement | Raw data, settings, run log, live time, controls and background measurements |
| Completion | Working apparatus, scientific and financial reports, reproducible analysis, operating guide, presentation/poster and individual contribution statements |

The instructor may request revisions before an award or before measurement. A justified change of plan is acceptable. Record what changed and why; do not rewrite the original proposal as if the change had been planned.

## 6. Teamwork and research practice

Assign primary and supporting roles. Every student must participate in commissioning, discuss the physics and uncertainties, and contribute to communication. Use a task log and signed contribution statements; commit counts alone do not establish contribution.

Identify electrical, mechanical, chemical and radiation-related hazards relevant to your apparatus. PMT high voltage, heavy lead components, strong magnets and tools require appropriate instructor procedures. Any use of radioactive sources requires separate instructor authorization and applicable institutional arrangements; access to scintillators does not imply access to sources. Record the required training and checks before work.

Cite borrowed designs, code, figures and text. Disclose AI assistance and verify its outputs. Never present fabricated or simulated observations as measurements. Agree on repository visibility and data handling with the instructor.

## 7. Assessment

| Component | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Proposal and feasibility | 15% |
| Apparatus and commissioning | 25% |
| Measurement, analysis and physics interpretation | 30% |
| Documentation and dissemination | 10% |
| Individual understanding and contribution | 20% |

See [assessment descriptors](ASSESSMENT.md). The first four components are team marks; the last is individual. Expected results are not guaranteed. A well-investigated limitation may earn strong marks; unsupported claims cannot. Equipment expenditure and visual polish do not compensate for weak physics.
